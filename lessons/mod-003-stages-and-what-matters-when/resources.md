*stage axis* — the clock on top of mod-002's map; the citations below
are the canonical entry points when you eventually need depth on any
specific stage or transition.

## The IDEA / PRE-SEED reconciliation (chapter 04)

- **Paul Graham, `Do Things That Don't Scale`**, July 2013 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/ds.html>. The canonical essay every
  IDEA / PRE-SEED founder in this org is expected to have read.
  Argues that the un-scalable manual work at the earliest stage is
  *how* the eventually-scalable business gets found, not a
  compromise around it.

## The stage-relevant methodology tradition (chapters 01-03)

- **Steve Blank, `The Four Steps to the Epiphany`**, 2005
  (self-published; later editions). Blank's Customer Development
  framework decomposes into four stages (Customer Discovery →
  Customer Validation → Customer Creation → Company Building) that
  align approximately with this module's IDEA → PRE-SEED → SEED →
  SERIES-A ladder. Referenced in mod-001 chapter 04, mod-002 chapter
  02, and this module's chapter 01.
- **Steve Blank and Bob Dorf, `The Startup Owner's Manual`**, K&S
  Ranch, 2012. The textbook-length expansion of Customer Development;
  the stage-transition signals across the Discovery / Validation /
  Creation / Building progression are the closest thing in the
  primary literature to the signal-column of this module's chapter
  02.
- **Eric Ries, `The Lean Startup`**, Crown Business, 2011. The
  Build-Measure-Learn loop is the operating shape of the earliest
  stages (chapter 04 references this). Ries's "pivot vs persevere"
  language is what a founder uses when the stage-transition signal
  fails to appear.
- **Ash Maurya, `Running Lean`** (2010, multiple editions) and the
  companion **`Scaling Lean`** (2016). Maurya's stage progression
  is more compressed than Blank's (Problem/Solution Fit →
  Product/Market Fit → Scale) but maps directly onto the ladder in
  this module.

## Stage-specific reference material (referenced across chapters)

### IDEA / PRE-SEED

- Y Combinator's essays and their **Startup School** materials —
  <https://www.startupschool.org>. Free curriculum covering many of
  the IDEA / PRE-SEED-stage activities. The signal a founder is at
  IDEA vs. PRE-SEED is often clearest when compared against YC's
  batch entry criteria.

### SEED

- **Y Combinator, the SAFE documents** —
  <https://www.ycombinator.com/documents>. Referenced in mod-002
  chapter 03; also relevant here as the market-standard SEED-stage
  fundraising instrument.
- **First Round Review** — <https://review.firstround.com>. Deep
  archive of first-hand founder essays especially strong on the
  SEED and SERIES-A transitions.

### SERIES-A

- **National Venture Capital Association, the NVCA Model Legal
  Documents** — <https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/>. The
  industry-standard priced-round documents; the SERIES-A signal on
  chapter 02's ladder is calibrated against what an NVCA-modeled
  Series A actually looks like.
- **Bessemer Venture Partners, `State of the Cloud`** and their
  benchmark reports — <https://www.bvp.com/atlas>. SaaS
  benchmarks that anchor "PMF signals a fund will underwrite."

### GROWTH

- **Andy Rachleff and the "product/market fit graduation" essays**
  (widely republished across First Round and other venture blogs).
  The GROWTH signal on chapter 02's ladder is calibrated against
  Rachleff's framing.

## How to read this list

- **Chapters 04 and 05 are primary-source chapters.** Read the
  primary essay / report *before* the paired exercise. The
  chapters are scaffolding; the exercises expect you to have the
  primary source open.
- **The methodology tradition (Blank, Ries, Maurya) is inherited
  from mod-001.** If you have not yet read mod-001 chapters 04 and
  05 and mod-002 chapter 02, do that first — those chapters install
  the vocabulary that this module's stage ladder aligns against.
- **The stage-specific reference material below is meant for
  filtering.** Do not attempt to read all of it. When you locate
  your own company at a specific stage using chapter 02, the
  corresponding block above is your reading list for that stage.
  When you transition stages, add the next block.
- **The Startup Genome report is short.** Even the follow-up is
  under 50 pages. Reading it directly is a small investment
  relative to reading a paraphrase; the specific dimensions and
  the specific headline number matter.

## Citation discipline for your own artifacts

When you write up an exercise or a project in this module and
reference a claim from one of the resources above, cite it in the
form Foundations has established:

- Graham, `Do Things That Don't Scale` (July 2013), para. N.
- Startup Genome Report (2011 or later edition — name it), section N.
- Blank, `The Four Steps to the Epiphany` (2005), ch. N.
- CB Insights, `Top 12 Reasons Startups Fail` (2021 update or
  applicable edition — name it), reason #N.
- Founder Collective, mortemas: [company name] post-mortem
  (author, year), URL.
- `STARTUP_STAGES.md` (root of `startup-foundations`), current
  commit hash if the specific wording matters.

Consistent citation is the discipline mod-001 established and every
downstream module in the org relies on. It is especially
load-bearing in this module because a stage claim is only
defensible if the signal-column evidence is cited to a source that
another reader can verify.
