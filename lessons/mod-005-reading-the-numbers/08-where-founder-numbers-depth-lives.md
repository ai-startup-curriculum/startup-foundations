# Where Founder-Numbers Depth Lives

## Motivation

Chapters 01–07 taught the founder-numbers slice — the small set of
numbers a PRE-SEED or SEED founder has to be able to read, weekly,
without a calculator: cash, burn (three variants), growth rate,
runway, the default-alive / default-dead composite, four unit-
economics terms at the vocabulary level, and one North-Star metric.

That is a deliberately narrow slice. It is not "everything a
founder should know about the numbers side of the business." Full
financial modelling, sensitivity analysis, capital-allocation
frameworks, unit-economics depth, and fundraising financial
packages are all real and important — and all owned by the
finance-fundraising pillar, not by Foundations.

This chapter is the deferral map. It follows the same shape as
mod-004 chapter 07 (founder-operating deferrals) and mod-002
chapter 07 (graph-level deferrals): name what this module owns,
name every adjacent depth, point at the owning pillar, and
explain when the pillar becomes load-bearing.

## The founder-numbers slice — what this module actually owns

This module owns, and only owns, four things:

1. **The runway model at PRE-SEED / SEED shape** — a spreadsheet
   with a starting balance, a monthly burn, a forward plan, and a
   months-of-runway output (chapter 01).
2. **The three burn variants** — gross, net, net-after-variable-
   revenue — and the self-deceptions each hides (chapter 02).
3. **The growth-rate mindset** — WoW / MoM cadence, the Graham
   yardstick, the failure modes of rate calculation (chapter 03) —
   and the default-alive / default-dead composite that fuses growth
   and burn into a survival call (chapter 04).
4. **The founder-numbers one-pager** — six headline rows, three-
   value grammar, weekly cadence, dual internal / external
   audience (chapter 07). Plus the North-Star metric selection
   process (chapter 06) that produces one of those rows, and the
   four unit-economic terms at vocabulary level (chapter 05).

Everything else — and there is a lot of "everything else" — is
depth in an owning pillar. The rest of this chapter walks the
deferrals.

## Deferral 1 — full financial modelling and the three statements

**What this module says.** Chapter 01 teaches a single-statement
model: a cash-flow projection from a starting balance, month by
month, with a forward plan of hires and spend. That is enough to
compute runway and default-alive / default-dead for a PRE-SEED /
SEED company.

**What this module does NOT teach.**

- The **three-statement financial model** — the interlocked income
  statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow statement that a mature
  company runs against.
- **Accrual accounting** — how revenue is recognised, how expenses
  are matched, how deferred revenue and accounts receivable flow
  through the books.
- **Working-capital modelling** — how changes in accounts
  receivable, accounts payable, and inventory affect cash-flow
  independently of profit-and-loss.
- **Bottom-up revenue models** — building revenue up from
  segments, cohorts, and channels rather than projecting a single
  growth rate against a single number.
- **Financial statement analysis** — reading a public or private
  company's actual statements and making judgments about the
  business from them.

**Where depth lives.**
[`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum)
(level 20). The finance-fundraising pillar owns the full modelling
stack, from the three statements up through the segmented
bottom-up revenue models and working-capital dynamics.

**When to reach for the pillar.** The pillar becomes load-bearing
around late SEED / early SERIES-A — when there is enough revenue and
enough segmentation that a single-line projection stops being
adequate, and when the company is about to talk to a fund or an
auditor who expects three statements. Foundations mod-005 gets you
to that point; the pillar takes you from there.

## Deferral 2 — sensitivity analysis and scenario modelling at depth

**What this module says.** Chapter 04 shows the default-alive /
default-dead composite run against a base case and one or two
variations (a growth cut, a burn cut). That is enough to see the
composite's sensitivity to its inputs and to understand what a
scenario is.

**What this module does NOT teach.**

- **Monte Carlo simulation** on the inputs — running the composite
  across a full probability distribution of growth, burn, and
  capital-timing outcomes.
- **Tornado charts** and formal sensitivity analysis — quantifying
  which inputs most move the outcome, in what direction, with what
  confidence.
- **Scenario planning as a strategic tool** — building named
  scenarios ("recession," "regulatory change," "competitor lands
  large customer") and running the model against each one.
- **Break-even and stress-testing** — finding the smallest input
  changes that flip the verdict, and the plans that defend against
  each.

**Where depth lives.** Same pillar —
[`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum).
Sensitivity and scenario tooling live alongside the modelling depth
in that pillar; the two topics are usually taught together.

**When to reach for the pillar.** Same rough transition as
Deferral 1 — late SEED / early SERIES-A. The signal is that the
founder is running a fundraise with a real financial model rather
than a founder-numbers one-pager, and investors are asking scenario
questions.

## Deferral 3 — unit economics at depth

**What this module says.** Chapter 05 names the four unit-
economics terms — CAC, LTV, payback, gross margin — at the
vocabulary level, names the definitional variants of each, and
names the specific way each is mis-used.

**What this module does NOT teach.**

- **Cohort analysis** — the actual methodology of tracking a group
  of customers over time and computing each of the four terms
  against real cohort data.
- **Multi-touch attribution** — how to assign CAC across channels
  when a customer's journey touched several.
- **LTV modelling by segment** — computing distinct LTVs for
  distinct customer segments and reasoning about which segments to
  invest in.
- **Payback optimisation** — the specific levers (pricing changes,
  packaging, onboarding, contract term) that move payback and the
  trade-offs between them.
- **Gross-margin engineering** — the specific moves (infrastructure
  choices, vendor negotiations, product-design decisions) that move
  gross margin, and the trade-off between margin and speed.
- **The unit-economics dashboard architecture** — how the four
  terms tie together into one operating dashboard the GTM / finance
  operating loop runs against.

**Where depth lives.**
[`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum),
specifically the unit-economics module. That module is authored
against the assumption that the reader can already name the four
terms — Foundations mod-005 chapter 05 is what makes that
assumption reasonable.

**When to reach for the pillar.** The unit-economics pillar module
becomes load-bearing around SEED-to-SERIES-A, when the company has
enough customers over a long enough period that real cohort
analysis is possible and the four terms move from "vocabulary" to
"weekly-tracked."

## Deferral 4 — capital-allocation frameworks

**What this module says.** Chapter 01's forward plan section
implies capital allocation at a coarse level — hires vs. non-hire
spend, this month vs. next month. Chapter 04's three levers for
flipping default dead → alive (cut burn, raise growth, raise
capital) frames capital as one lever alongside the others. That is
the full extent.

**What this module does NOT teach.**

- The **capital-allocation framework** by which a mature company
  decides how much of its capital goes to which use (product vs.
  GTM vs. R&D vs. corp-dev) on which timeline against which
  expected return.
- **ROI-driven allocation** — quantifying the expected return of
  each candidate use of capital and picking the highest-ROI mix.
- **Portfolio thinking** — treating different investment areas
  (segments, markets, product lines) as a portfolio with different
  risk profiles and expected returns.
- **The capital-allocation cadence** — how a mature company runs
  the recurring meeting rhythm through which allocation decisions
  are re-visited and updated.

**Where depth lives.**
[`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum).
Capital-allocation frameworks are finance-pillar depth.

**When to reach for the pillar.** Later than the previous
deferrals — usually GROWTH stage. At PRE-SEED / SEED, capital is
scarce enough and the operating team is small enough that
allocation-as-a-framework is stage-mismatched. The founder makes
each spend call individually, logs it in mod-004, and re-runs the
composite in chapter 04. Capital-allocation as a systematic
framework becomes real once there are meaningful discretionary
dollars and multiple viable uses of them.

## Deferral 5 — fundraising financial packages

**What this module says.** The founder-numbers one-pager in
chapter 07 is honest enough to hand to a candidate investor and
correct enough to lead a fundraise conversation. That is the
Foundations shape.

**What this module does NOT teach.**

- The **fundraise financial package** — the detailed set of
  documents a fund expects: a full three-statement model, cohort
  data, unit-economics tables, a build-plan for the runway of the
  round, a pricing model, and the specific artifacts each investor
  type asks for.
- **Round pricing and terms** — how a SAFE, a priced round, and
  the various clauses (pro-rata, MFN, valuation caps, discount
  rates) affect the numbers.
- **Fundraise process** — how the outreach cadence, the meeting
  sequence, the term-sheet negotiation, and the closing process
  are run.
- **Cap-table modelling** — how each round, each option grant,
  and each conversion affects the ownership distribution and the
  dilution profile.
- **The fundraise narrative-and-numbers pairing** — how the
  Sequoia-shape narrative (mod-004 chapter 05) and the fundraise
  financial package fit together into a coherent fundraise story.

**Where depth lives.**
[`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum).
The whole fundraising process, including the specific financial
package, is finance-pillar depth.

**Adjacent depth.** The Sequoia-shape narrative itself is mod-004
depth (chapter 05 of that module). The founder-narrative and the
fundraise financial package meet in the fundraise; each is owned
by a different curriculum position, and both are read by
finance-fundraising when the actual round is run.

**When to reach for the pillar.** The signal is a real fundraise
starting. Foundations mod-005 gets you to *have the numbers to talk
about*; the fundraise pillar takes you through the process of
turning that into a closed round.

## The deferral formula in this module

mod-002 chapter 07 defined the deferral formula Foundations uses
throughout:

> `<Node>` — position on the graph, brief description of what the
> node is, and hand-off. **Depth lives in `<owning repo>`.**
> Foundations does not go further; do not go read the deep
> material until it is stage-appropriate for your work.

This module uses the formula against every topic listed above. When
a chapter mentions a three-statement model, a cohort, a capital-
allocation framework, or a fundraise package, it names the topic,
connects it to the founder-numbers slice this module owns, and
points at the finance-fundraising pillar. No chapter tries to be
the pillar; every chapter points at the pillar it needs.

## Why the boundary matters

Three reasons, the same three reasons mod-004 chapter 07 named:

**1. This module has to be finish-able at PRE-SEED / SEED.** A
pre-seed founder needs runway, burn, growth, and a default-alive /
default-dead call *now*; they cannot wait for a three-statement
financial-modelling module. The boundary is what makes mod-005 28
hours instead of 280.

**2. The pillar work is stage-gated too.** A pre-seed founder does
not need the full finance-fundraising pillar; they need the
one-pager and the composite. Sending them to the whole pillar too
early is stage-mismatched activity per mod-003.

**3. The pillar keeps a single source of truth.** When the SAFE
form updates, when accounting standards change, when the SaaS
gross-margin conventions shift because AI infrastructure costs
have re-shaped the industry, the updates land in
[`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum).
This module's job is to point to the pillar, not to duplicate the
depth — so it never falls out of date on topics that change faster
than Foundations does.

## Summary

- This module owns the **founder-numbers slice**: runway model,
  three burns, growth rate, default-alive / default-dead composite,
  North-Star selection, and the six-line one-pager. Nothing more.
- **Full financial modelling** (three statements, accrual accounting,
  working capital, bottom-up revenue modelling) lives in
  [`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum).
- **Sensitivity, scenario, and Monte Carlo analysis** live in the
  same pillar.
- **Unit-economics depth** (cohort analysis, LTV by segment,
  payback optimisation, gross-margin engineering) lives in the same
  pillar.
- **Capital-allocation frameworks** live in the same pillar.
- **Fundraise financial packages, cap-table modelling, round
  pricing** live in the same pillar.
- The boundary is what keeps this module tractable and keeps the
  pillar authoritative.

## Homework

This chapter has no dedicated exercise. Its content is exercised
implicitly in every other exercise in the module — when an
exercise surfaces a topic beyond the founder-numbers slice (a
cohort question, a scenario question, a fundraise package
question), the writer is expected to name the depth as pillar-owned
and stop there. The exercise reviewers (and the module quiz, when
written) check that the boundary is respected.
