# Resources — mod-005 Reading the Numbers

The primary sources this module builds on. Foundations only teaches
the *founder-numbers slice* — the small set of numbers a PRE-SEED /
SEED founder has to be able to read weekly without a calculator —
and the citations below are the canonical entry points for each of
those numbers. Depth-level modelling, sensitivity analysis, unit-
economics engineering, capital-allocation, and fundraise-package
material live in `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`; see
chapter 08 for the deferral map.

## Runway, burn, and the composite (chapters 01, 02, 04)

The runway model at PRE-SEED / SEED shape and the default-alive /
default-dead composite are the module's two most consequential
artifacts. Both trace back to a single canonical essay.

- **Paul Graham, `Default Alive or Default Dead?`**, October 2015 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/aord.html>. The founding essay for the
  composite call in chapter 04 — given current growth, current
  revenue trajectory, current spend, and current cash, does the
  startup reach profitability before the money runs out?
  Referenced across chapters 01 (why runway needs a verdict
  attached), 02 (why the three burn variants matter), and 04 (the
  composite itself). Read the essay in full before exercise 04.

## Growth as a rate (chapter 03)

Chapter 03's "growth as a rate" mindset and the specific
5–7% WoW yardstick trace back to a single Paul Graham essay
that mod-001 also references.

- **Paul Graham, `Startup = Growth`**, September 2012 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/growth.html>. The canonical statement
  that a startup is a company designed to grow fast, with the
  widely-cited weekly growth-rate yardsticks. Referenced across
  the module and across the org. Read the essay in full before
  exercise 03; the specific numeric yardsticks matter and are
  easily distorted in secondary summaries.
- **Sam Altman, `Startup Playbook`** —
  <https://playbook.samaltman.com>. Referenced adjacent to the
  Graham yardstick as the working-founder framing of the same
  growth-rate discipline. The playbook is short and pairs well
  with `Startup = Growth`.

## The unit-economics vocabulary (chapter 05)

Chapter 05 introduces CAC, LTV, payback, and gross margin at the
vocabulary level only; depth lives in the finance-fundraising
pillar. The references below are enough to define each of the
four terms accurately without pulling the learner into the pillar.

- **David Skok, `SaaStr` and `For Entrepreneurs` writing on SaaS
  metrics** — <https://www.forentrepreneurs.com>. Skok's blog is
  the widely-cited canonical primer on CAC, LTV, payback, and
  gross margin for SaaS businesses; the "SaaS Metrics 2.0"
  series is the entry point.
  <!-- needs-research: locate direct URLs for the specific "SaaS Metrics 2.0" essays on each of CAC, LTV, payback, and gross margin; forentrepreneurs.com has re-organised its archives multiple times. -->
- **Andrew Chen, on CAC and growth loops** — Andrew Chen's blog
  at <https://andrewchen.com>. Complementary writing on CAC and
  the acquisition side of unit economics, especially for
  marketplace and consumer products where SaaS conventions don't
  cleanly apply.
- **Bessemer Venture Partners, `State of the Cloud` and related
  benchmark reports** — <https://www.bvp.com/atlas>. Referenced
  in mod-003 as the SEED / SERIES-A benchmark set; useful here
  as the source of the SaaS-convention numbers a founder is
  benchmarking against when the four terms move from vocabulary
  to weekly-tracked.
- **Tomasz Tunguz, on SaaS unit economics** —
  <https://tomtunguz.com>. Working-VC writing on the four terms
  in context; complements Skok with an investor-side voice.

## How to read this list

- **Chapters 03, 04, 05, 06, and 07 are primary-source chapters.**
  Read the primary reference *before* the paired exercise. The
  chapters are scaffolding; the exercises expect you to have the
  primary source open.
- **The two Paul Graham essays are short.** `Startup = Growth`
  and `Default Alive or Default Dead?` together take under two
  hours to read carefully; both are cited across the whole
  Foundations curriculum and both reward re-reading against your
  specific numbers.
- **The Amplitude North Star Playbook is not required in full.**
  The first two chapters and the metric-selection worksheet are
  the parts that matter for chapter 06 and exercise 06; the rest
  is depth for a product-analytics team the founder does not yet
  have.
- **The David Skok SaaS Metrics 2.0 series is optional.** Chapter
  05 is explicit that the four terms are introduced at the
  vocabulary level only. If your startup is SEED or beyond and
  one of the four is becoming load-bearing, read the Skok essay
  on that specific term; otherwise, save the reading for the
  pillar module.
- **The YC and Fred Wilson short-form writing is meant to be
  read in one sitting.** These are essays and blog posts, not
  books. If a reading of chapter 07 leaves you unsure about the
  90-second-read discipline, the AVC posts and the YC primer are
  the right next thirty minutes.

## Citation discipline for your own artifacts

When you write up an exercise or a project in this module and
reference a claim from one of the resources above, cite it in the
form Foundations has established:

- Graham, `Default Alive or Default Dead?` (October 2015), para. N.
- Graham, `Startup = Growth` (September 2012), para. N.
- Amplitude, `The North Star Playbook` (current edition), ch. N.
- Skok, `SaaS Metrics 2.0 — Detailed Definitions` (year, URL).
- YC, `The Numbers of a Startup` (URL, retrieval date).
- mod-004 chapter 02, section "The metrics one-pager" (specific
  commit hash if the exact wording matters).

Consistent citation is the discipline mod-001 established and
every downstream module in the org relies on. It matters most in
this module for chapters 03, 04, and 06 — the growth-rate
yardstick, the default-alive / default-dead composite, and the
North-Star selection each depend on specific claims from named
primary sources, and paraphrasing without citation loses the
audit trail a candidate investor will eventually check.
