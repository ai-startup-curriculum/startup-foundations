# Resources — mod-002 The Dependency Graph

The primary sources this module builds on. Foundations only teaches the
graph's *positions*; the citations below are the canonical entry points
when you eventually need depth on any node.

## The two canvases (chapter 06)

- Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur, **`Business Model Generation`**,
  Wiley, 2010. The founding text of the Business Model Canvas. The
  BMC is the reference frame most non-startup organisations use to
  describe a business model on one page.
- Strategyzer (Osterwalder's company), **the BMC canvas and
  supporting resources** — <https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas>.
  Canonical publisher-endorsed template and companion materials.

- Ash Maurya, **`Running Lean`**, O'Reilly, 2010 (multiple later
  editions). The founding text of the Lean Canvas — Maurya's
  Lean-Startup-oriented adaptation of Osterwalder's canvas that
  swaps in Problem / Solution / Key Metrics / Unfair Advantage in
  place of Key Activities / Key Resources / Key Partnerships /
  Customer Relationships.
- Ash Maurya's LeanStack — <https://leanstack.com/lean-canvas>.
  Canonical Lean Canvas landing page and template.

## The corporate-structure branch (chapter 03)

### Incorporation

- Cooley GO — <https://www.cooleygo.com>. Cooley LLP's public
  startup-oriented resource; standard reference for U.S. C-corp
  incorporation, founder agreements, and early-stage legal
  hygiene.
- Wilson Sonsini — <https://www.wsgr.com>. WSGR's public materials
  on incorporation, founder-agreement basics, and diligence
  checklists.
- Gunderson Dettmer — <https://www.gunder.com>. Gunderson's
  startup-focused legal resources; the third firm most commonly
  referenced by early-stage founders in the U.S. venture context.
  <!-- needs-research: for each of the three firms above, identify the specific public guide most commonly cited by founders as their "getting-started incorporation" reference and link directly rather than to the firm homepage. -->

### Equity + Fundraising

- Y Combinator, **the SAFE documents** —
  <https://www.ycombinator.com/documents>. The market-standard
  early convertible instrument (originally 2013; revised to a
  post-money form in 2018). YC also publishes the accompanying
  User Guide and side-letter templates.
- National Venture Capital Association, **the NVCA Model Legal
  Documents** — <https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/>. The
  industry-standard starting point for priced venture rounds
  from seed onward.
- Brad Feld and Jason Mendelson, **`Venture Deals`**, Wiley (multiple
  editions). The plain-English primer on term sheets, valuations,
  and the mechanics of the priced round. Frequently cited as the
  single book to hand a first-time founder before their first
  term-sheet negotiation.
- Carta, **the Carta State of Private Markets** —
  <https://carta.com>. Recurring quantitative report on cap-table
  and equity metrics across venture-backed companies; useful for
  benchmarking pool sizes, dilution patterns, and round terms.
  <!-- needs-research: identify the current Carta report edition and cite direct URLs to specific data points (option pool at Series A, dilution at seed, etc.) before quoting specific percentages in learner content. -->

### Governance

- **Delaware General Corporation Law** — the governing statute for
  most U.S. venture-backed C-corps. Not required first-pass
  reading, but the canonical legal reference for board authority,
  fiduciary duty (including the *Revlon* line of duties on
  change-of-control), and shareholder rights.
  <!-- needs-research: cite an accessible plain-English primer on Revlon duties and their applicability to startup M&A (both Wilson Sonsini and Cooley publish such primers). -->
- **NACD (National Association of Corporate Directors)** guidance
  on board practice — <https://www.nacdonline.org>. Reference for
  board-pack shape and board-meeting mechanics as companies
  mature.

## The economics branch (chapter 04)

## How to read this list

- **Chapter 02–04 are branch tours.** Each cites the depth-owner's
  canonical text. You do not need to read the entire canonical
  text to complete the exercises; the acceptance criteria in
  each exercise name the minimum reading depth.
- **Chapter 06 is the canvas chapter.** Both canvas books are
  fast reads and worth the whole first pass. The strategyzer.com
  and leanstack.com landing pages are the fastest way to see the
  canvases themselves; the books are where the *reasoning* lives.
- **Chapter 07 is the ownership rule.** The resources are the
  pillar-curriculum repos themselves — go browse each one so
  when you route a founder question, you know what they'll find
  there.

## Citation discipline for your own artifacts

When you write up an exercise or a project in this module and
reference a claim from one of the resources above, cite it in this
form:

- Maurya, *Running Lean* (2010), ch. 3.
- Osterwalder & Pigneur, *Business Model Generation* (2010), p. N.
- Graham, `Default Alive or Default Dead?` (October 2015), para. N.
- YC SAFE User Guide (2018 post-money version), section N.
- NVCA Model Certificate of Incorporation (current version), § N.

Consistent citation is the same discipline mod-001 established
and every downstream module in the org relies on. It also makes
your own artifacts *reusable* — a placement document with proper
citations is one a co-founder or new hire can inherit without
having to re-verify every claim.
