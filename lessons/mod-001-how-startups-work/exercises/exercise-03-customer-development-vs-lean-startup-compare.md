# Exercise 03 — Customer Development vs Lean Startup — Compare

## Problem statement

Chapter 04 introduced the two operating traditions Foundations sits on top
of — Steve Blank's Customer Development and Eric Ries's Lean Startup. Both
are widely taught, widely referenced, and widely conflated. This exercise
makes you distinguish them precisely, on a specific set of dimensions,
using the primary sources.

The point is not to pick a winner. The point is to be able to look at any
technique you encounter later — a landing-page smoke test, a cohort
retention chart, a persona interview, a pricing experiment — and name
which tradition it comes from, at which stage of that tradition it lives,
and what its complementary practice from the other tradition would be.

## Requirements

1. **Read the primary sources.** At minimum, read the introduction and
   first two chapters of each. Skimming later chapters is acceptable if
   the first read tells you what you need. Sources:
   - Steve Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* — or the more
     tutorial-shaped Blank & Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual* (2012).
     Public writing: <https://steveblank.com>.
   - Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup* (Crown Business, 2011). Public site:
     <http://theleanstartup.com>.

3. **Write a "how they stack" section.** Chapter 04 argues that Blank's
   stages are the outer arc and Ries's loop is the tempo inside each
   stage. Confirm or push back on that claim in one paragraph, using
   evidence from the primary sources. If you push back, name a specific
   place where the two traditions actually contradict rather than
   complement each other.

4. **Trace two real techniques to their tradition.** Pick two techniques
   from the list below (or two of your own) and, for each: name the
   tradition it comes from, name the stage or loop step it lives in, and
   name the complementary practice from the *other* tradition that a
   thorough founder would pair it with.

   Suggested techniques:
   - A smoke-test landing page with a signup form.
   - A structured customer interview using an open-ended pain-point
     script.
   - A cohort retention chart broken out by week 1, week 4, and week 12.
   - A price-sensitivity survey (e.g., Van Westendorp).
   - A weekly Build-Measure-Learn cycle timebox.
   - A jobs-to-be-done conversation.
   - A concierge MVP (the founder manually delivers the product to
     early customers).
   - An A/B test on a pricing page.

5. **Write a stage-fit note.** In one paragraph, argue which tradition is
   more load-bearing at each stage on the ladder (IDEA, PRE-SEED, SEED,
   SERIES-A). This is a judgement call — defend yours.

## Starter guidance

- **Blank is older; Ries builds on Blank.** Ries explicitly credits Blank
  as a teacher. Do not present them as competitors; they are more like a
  strategy layer and a tempo layer.
- **The MVP concept is Ries's, but Blank talks about early customers.**
  Watch for the distinction — an MVP is a *product artifact designed to
  test a hypothesis*; Blank's early customer conversations are about
  learning *without* a product. They are complementary tools, not
  duplicates.
- **Pivot is a Ries term.** Blank has an equivalent (a re-visit of a
  Customer Discovery hypothesis) but not the sharp vocabulary. When you
  fill the "how does a founder change direction" row, note whose term is
  whose.
- **Both traditions have evolved.** Ries's later work (`The Startup Way`)
  extends Lean Startup to large organizations; Blank has evolved the
  Customer Development framing in his later blog posts and in the Startup
  Owner's Manual. When there's tension between the original book and
  later refinements, quote both and note which you're using.
- **Do not source the traditions from third-party summaries.** Blog-post
  explainers of the two traditions are commonly wrong in specific ways
  (they conflate MVP with "first version of the product," or they collapse
  Customer Development into "talk to customers"). Read the primary
  sources.

## Acceptance criteria

You have completed the exercise when:

- [ ] Both primary sources have been read at least through introduction
      and first two chapters.
- [ ] The comparison table has every cell filled with specific,
      attributable content.
- [ ] The "how they stack" section confirms or pushes back on chapter
      04's stacking claim with reference to primary sources.
- [ ] At least two real techniques are traced to their tradition, stage,
      and complementary practice.
- [ ] The stage-fit paragraph names which tradition is more load-bearing
      at each of IDEA, PRE-SEED, SEED, SERIES-A.
- [ ] References section cites the specific chapters or sections read.
- [ ] The file is committed as
      `mod-001-exercise-03-customer-development-vs-lean-startup.md`.

## Common failure modes to avoid

- Presenting the two as competitors and picking a winner. They are not
  competitors.
- Sourcing the traditions from Wikipedia-style summaries instead of the
  books. The summaries are often wrong.
- Filling the "weakest" row with anodyne caveats. Push — where does each
  tradition actually mislead a naïve reader?
- Treating "MVP" and "first release" as synonyms. Ries is emphatic that
  they are not. Get this distinction right in your comparison.
- Writing a book report on each in parallel instead of a comparison.

## What good looks like

A comparison sharp enough that a founder who reads it can, when they
encounter a technique in a later curriculum, immediately place it and know
what to pair it with. The two-column table is the artifact — a stranger
should be able to skim it in two minutes and know the difference.

You will reuse this comparison when you get to
`startup-product-gtm-curriculum` (level 20), where the discovery and PMF
modules assume you already know which tradition supplied which piece of
vocabulary.
