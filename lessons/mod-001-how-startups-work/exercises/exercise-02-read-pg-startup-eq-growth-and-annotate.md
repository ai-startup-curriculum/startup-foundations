# Exercise 02 — Read `Startup = Growth` and Annotate

## Problem statement

Paul Graham's `Startup = Growth` (September 2012) is the single most-cited
essay in the ai-startup-curriculum org. Every downstream pillar assumes you
have read it and internalized the growth-as-compass framing. This exercise
makes sure you have.

You will read the essay in full, produce a structured annotation, extract
the load-bearing claims, and then argue with the essay in one place. The
last step is the point — passive reading of the canon is unlicensed
reading; you have to be able to *use* the argument, which means being able
to disagree with it in specific places.

## Requirements

1. **Read the primary essay in full.** Do not skim. Do not rely on
   summaries. Do not rely on chapter 02 of this module — that chapter is a
   scaffold, not a substitute. Source:
   <https://paulgraham.com/growth.html>.

2. **Produce a structured annotation** of the essay in Markdown with the
   following sections:

   - **Thesis in one sentence.** State the essay's central claim in your
     own words. If it takes you more than one sentence, keep trying.
   - **Five load-bearing claims.** Extract the five claims the argument
     rests on, in the order Graham makes them. For each: a one-sentence
     paraphrase in your own words, plus a direct quote (2–3 sentences
     maximum) from the essay that grounds the paraphrase. Cite the quote
     location by paragraph if the essay text is unnumbered.
   - **The growth-rate yardsticks.** Extract the numerical growth-rate
     yardsticks the essay uses (the weekly percentages it names as good,
     strong, and warning). Quote them exactly. These are the numbers
     Foundations returns to in mod-005 — get them right.
   - **How Graham distinguishes startups from other new businesses.**
     One paragraph. This is where the essay complements chapter 01.
   - **How Graham says a founder should read growth.** One paragraph on
     what growth *does* for the founder — as a compass, a diagnostic, and
     a filter.

3. **Argue with the essay.** In a section titled `Disagreements and
   caveats`, write at least **two specific disagreements or caveats** — not
   with the essay's overall thesis, but with a specific claim the essay
   makes. Each disagreement should be at least a paragraph and should:
   - Quote or paraphrase the specific claim you're pushing back on.
   - Name the case in which Graham's claim breaks down or is misleading.
   - Not resolve the disagreement by pretending Graham didn't mean what he
     said. Steelman first, then push back.

   Common productive disagreements: enterprise businesses with long sales
   cycles; hardware; regulated markets; two-sided marketplaces before
   liquidity; deep-tech startups where the search for validated growth
   takes years; startups where growth is present but is a vanity metric
   (retention is the real hole).

4. **Cross-reference with chapter 01.** In a short section, note where the
   Graham essay complements the Blank definition (chapter 01) and where it
   adds something the Blank definition does not (specifically, the growth
   rate as the compass variable).

5. **Cross-reference with chapter 05.** In a short section, note which of
   the five searches (problem, solution, market, business model, team) the
   growth rate is most directly diagnostic of.

## Starter guidance

- **Read once for surface, then once for annotation.** Do not try to
  extract the five load-bearing claims on the first pass — you will miss
  half of them because you're still tracking the argument. First read
  cold; then read again with a pen (real or virtual).
- **Quote exactly. Do not paraphrase in quote marks.** The distinction
  between quote and paraphrase is a discipline; get it right here.
- **Steelman before you disagree.** A disagreement that misrepresents
  Graham's actual claim is worthless. Write out the strongest version of
  his claim first, then argue with *that* version.
- **The growth-rate yardsticks matter.** Get the numbers right — you will
  reference them in mod-005 (`Reading the Numbers`) and again in the
  founder-numbers packet project. If the essay's exact wording is
  ambiguous, quote it as-is and note the ambiguity.
- **Watch the year the essay was written.** 2012 was pre-widespread
  product-led-growth, pre-COVID, pre-generative-AI. Some claims are
  time-stamped. Note where the passage of time changes the picture.

## Acceptance criteria

You have completed the exercise when:

- [ ] The primary essay has been read in full (not just chapter 02).
- [ ] The one-sentence thesis is in your own words and is genuinely one
      sentence.
- [ ] Five load-bearing claims are extracted, each with a paraphrase, a
      direct quote, and a location reference.
- [ ] The growth-rate yardsticks table is filled with exact quoted values.
- [ ] The two cross-reference paragraphs (with chapter 01 and chapter 05)
      are present.
- [ ] At least two substantive, steelmanned disagreements are written,
      each at least a paragraph and each identifying a specific claim.
- [ ] The file is committed to your notes as
      `mod-001-exercise-02-startup-equals-growth-annotated.md`.

## Common failure modes to avoid

- Writing a book report. This is annotation and argument, not summary.
- Extracting five claims that are all versions of the same claim. Force
  variety — problem framing, growth-rate math, comparison to other
  businesses, founder implications, and market implications each count as
  distinct.
- Skipping the disagreements section because "Graham is right." Even if
  the essay is right in its core, specific claims break in specific
  cases. Find those cases.
- Cross-referencing the wrong chapter. Chapter 02 in this module is *the
  scaffold for the essay*; the cross-reference is to chapters 01 (the
  definition) and 05 (the search model). Foundations chapters, not this
  module's chapter 02.

## What good looks like

An annotated read that a first-time reader could use as a working guide
alongside the primary essay. They can see the load-bearing claims lifted
out, the growth-rate numbers quoted exactly, and the places where the
essay is time-stamped or context-dependent. Your two disagreements should
make them think, not roll their eyes.

You will reuse this annotation for mod-005 (where the 5–7% yardstick
becomes a first-class founder-number) and for exercise 04 (which asks you
to name the search that growth is diagnostic of).
