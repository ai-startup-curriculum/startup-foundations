# Resources — mod-001 How Startups Work

The primary sources this module builds on. When a chapter cites one of
these, read the primary source — the chapter is a scaffold, not a
substitute.

## Primary essays — Paul Graham

- Paul Graham, **`Startup = Growth`**, Y Combinator, September 2012 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/growth.html>. The canonical statement that a
  startup is a company designed to grow fast, with the widely-cited
  weekly growth-rate yardsticks. Referenced throughout chapters 02, 05,
  and mod-005.
- Paul Graham, **`How to Start a Startup`**, March 2005 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/start.html>. The three-ingredient argument
  (good people, something people want, spend little) that Foundations
  treats as the affirmative shape. Referenced in chapter 03.
- Paul Graham, **`The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups`**, October 2006 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html>. The companion failure-mode
  list; chapter 03 clusters the 18 into five graph-layer groups. Referenced
  in chapters 03 and 06 and in exercise 05.
- Paul Graham, **`Do Things That Don't Scale`**, July 2013 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/ds.html>. Cited in mod-003 as the canonical
  IDEA / PRE-SEED reconciliation; introduced here for completeness.
- Paul Graham, **`Default Alive or Default Dead?`**, October 2015 —
  <https://paulgraham.com/aord.html>. Cited in mod-005; introduced here
  because it complements `Startup = Growth` as the survival-check
  companion to the growth compass.

## Primary books — the two operating traditions

### Steve Blank — Customer Development

- Steve Blank, **`The Four Steps to the Epiphany`**, 2005
  (self-published; multiple later editions). The founding text of the
  Customer Development framework. Referenced in chapter 04.
- Steve Blank and Bob Dorf, **`The Startup Owner's Manual`**, K&S Ranch
  Publishing, 2012. The book-length, textbook-style expansion of
  Customer Development — often the more accessible entry point than the
  original *Four Steps*.
- Steve Blank's public writing: **<https://steveblank.com>**. Deep archive
  of posts on Customer Development, Lean LaunchPad, and the practical
  application of the framework across many contexts.

### Eric Ries — Lean Startup

- Eric Ries, **`The Lean Startup`**, Crown Business, 2011. The founding
  text of the Build-Measure-Learn loop, the MVP, and validated learning
  as the founder's unit of progress. Referenced in chapter 04 and
  throughout the org.
- Eric Ries, **`The Startup Way`**, Currency, 2017. Extends the Lean
  Startup framework to large organizations. Useful for founders who will
  eventually deal with enterprise customers running the same
  methodology internally.
- Public site: **<http://theleanstartup.com>**.

## How to read this list

- **Do the primary reading in the order the chapters cite it.** The
  chapters are ordered so that each primary source is introduced in
  context. Reading `The Lean Startup` in full before you've done
  chapter 01 will make sense; it just won't compound with the rest of
  the module the way the intended order does.
- **You do not need to read every book cover-to-cover.** The
  acceptance criteria in each exercise tell you the minimum reading
  depth (usually the introduction and first two chapters plus one
  targeted chapter). Do that first; go deeper if the material rewards
  the time.
- **Keep a running annotation file.** You will re-encounter the same
  quotes across mod-001, mod-002, mod-003, mod-004, and mod-005. An
  annotated copy of the Graham essays and a chapter-indexed summary
  of Blank and Ries pays off over the whole curriculum.

## Citation discipline for your own artifacts

When you write up an exercise or a project in this module and reference
a claim from one of the resources above, cite it in this form:

- Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* (2005), ch. 2.
- Graham, `Startup = Growth` (September 2012), para. 14.
- CB Insights, `The Top 12 Reasons Startups Fail` (year of edition
  used), reason #N.

Consistent citation makes your own artifacts reusable in later modules
and in the projects. It is the same discipline the rest of the org
uses.
