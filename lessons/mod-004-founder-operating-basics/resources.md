# Resources — mod-004 Founder Operating Basics

The primary sources this module builds on. Foundations only teaches
the *operating loop* and the smallest set of artifacts that carry it
— the citations below are the canonical entry points for the
depth-level material in each part of the loop (goal-setting,
narrative, weekly cadence, investor-update discipline). Depth on
each specific artifact lives in the pillar curricula named in
chapter 07 and at the end of this file.

## The operating loop and weekly cadence (chapters 01, 02)

The four-part loop (decide, allocate, communicate, measure) and
the Monday-plan / Friday-shipped / metrics-one-pager cadence are
scaffolds this module composes from more general management writing
rather than a single canonical source. The books below are the
primary references.

- **Andy Grove, `High Output Management`**, Random House, 1983
  (Vintage reprint 1995). The canonical text on the management
  practice the operating loop derives from — leverage as the unit of
  managerial output, one-on-ones, meetings as the medium of
  managerial work, and task-relevant maturity. Referenced across
  chapters 01 (why weekly beats ad-hoc), 02 (why the shipped-and-
  learned exists), and 04 (the OKR practice). Read chapters 3, 4,
  and 8 first if you're time-constrained.
- **Ben Horowitz, `The Hard Thing About Hard Things`**,
  HarperBusiness, 2014. The working founder-CEO memoir that
  complements Grove with the founder-specific failure modes the
  operating loop is meant to defend against. Referenced in mod-001
  as adjacent reading and in this module in chapter 01 for the
  founder-time-allocation argument.
- **Frederick Brooks, `The Mythical Man-Month`**, Addison-Wesley,
  1975 (Anniversary edition 1995). The single most-cited defence of
  written communication ("the plan" as an artifact) in software
  organisations. Chapter 03's decision log and chapter 02's Monday
  plan both inherit the Brooks argument that the written artifact
  is what survives the person who wrote it.

## The lightweight-OKR practice (chapter 04)

The two-person-scale OKR shape in chapter 04 is a deliberate
compression of the full OKR practice. The two primary references
are the ones every OKR conversation eventually traces back to.

- **Andy Grove, `High Output Management`** (see above). The origin
  of OKRs — Grove's "objectives and key results" practice at Intel
  in the 1970s. Chapter 4 of the book is the shortest primary
  reference; chapter 8 is the depth on leverage and how KRs earn
  their keep.
- **John Doerr, `Measure What Matters`**, Portfolio, 2018. The
  book-length treatment of OKRs — how Grove's practice moved from
  Intel through Google, Bono's ONE, and others. Chapter 04
  references Doerr for the "commit-and-review" cadence and for the
  anti-pattern catalogue (goals as activities, KRs as inputs,
  cascade-inflation of objectives).

## Adjacent works — worth reading, not required for this module

- **Peter Drucker, `The Effective Executive`**, Harper & Row,
  1967. The oldest of the canonical management texts and still
  the shortest primer on time-allocation and prioritisation
  discipline that chapter 01's *allocate* step derives from.
- **Marty Cagan, `Inspired`** (2nd ed., Wiley, 2017) and
  `Empowered` (Wiley, 2020). Product-operating-loop depth; the
  founder-loop of this module composes with the product
  operating loop these books formalise, once the org grows past
  founder-alone / founder-plus-one.
- **Jim Collins, `Good to Great`**, HarperBusiness, 2001. The
  Level 5 leadership and the "who before what" arguments are
  referenced across the founder literature; adjacent reading, not
  required.

## How to read this list

- **Chapters 04, 05, and 06 are primary-source chapters.** Read
  the primary reference *before* the paired exercise. The chapters
  are scaffolding; the exercises expect you to have the primary
  source open.
- **The management texts (Grove, Doerr, Drucker) reward slow
  reading.** They are decades old, still current, and cover
  material that later founder writing paraphrases without adding
  much. Even fifty pages of Grove is a better investment than most
  contemporary founder blog posts on the same topic.
- **The YC and SaaStr writing is short.** The investor-update
  primer references are essays and short blog posts, not books —
  read them in full rather than skimming.
- **The Sequoia template is the shape, not the argument.** Read
  the template to see the ten parts; read Graham's
  `How to Start a Startup` (mod-001 resources) for the argument
  the template's structure encodes.

## Citation discipline for your own artifacts

When you write up an exercise or a project in this module and
reference a claim from one of the resources above, cite it in the
form Foundations has established:

- Grove, *High Output Management* (1983), ch. N.
- Doerr, *Measure What Matters* (2018), ch. N.
- Sequoia Capital, `Writing a Business Plan` (current edition, URL).
- Y Combinator, `How to Write an Investor Update` (URL, retrieval
  date).
- SaaStr / Lemkin, `The Investor Update: A Template` (URL, retrieval
  date).
- First Round Review, `[Post title]` (author, year), URL.

Consistent citation is the discipline mod-001 established and
every downstream module in the org relies on. In this module it
matters most for chapter 05 (the Sequoia template — quote the
current live template, not a memory of it) and chapter 06 (the
investor-update primary references — the shape is widely
paraphrased and citing the source keeps the artifact honest).
