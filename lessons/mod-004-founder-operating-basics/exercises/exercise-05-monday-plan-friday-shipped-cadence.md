# Exercise 05 — Monday Plan / Friday Shipped-and-Learned Cadence

## Problem statement

Chapter 02 formalised the three weekly cadence artifacts — the
**Monday plan**, the **metrics one-pager**, and the **Friday
shipped-and-learned** — and named the interlocks between them.
Exercise 01 exercises the whole loop in one simulated week. This
exercise focuses specifically on the two cadence artifacts most
founders under-invest in — the Monday plan and the Friday
shipped-and-learned — over **two consecutive real weeks**.

Two weeks is the smallest window that lets you exercise the
**interlock**: the Friday of week 1 should meaningfully feed the
Monday of week 2. A single week can't test that interlock; a
month is more work than the exercise asks for.

## Requirements

### Part A — commit to two real weeks (about 5 min)

Pick two consecutive real work weeks over which you will run the
Monday plan / Friday shipped cadence for a real startup or a real
piece of work you own. Options:

- **Your own startup.** Best case; the exercise doubles as real
  operating value.
- **A startup you advise closely enough to be the operator for
  two weeks.** With their consent; the artifacts stay with them
  after the exercise.
- **A large project you own at your day job.** If you're not
  currently running a startup, a project you own where the
  Monday plan / Friday shipped shape maps naturally (a product
  launch, a research initiative, a major migration) is a
  legitimate substitute.

Simulated weeks are *not* an acceptable substitute for this
exercise — exercise 01 is the simulation exercise. Exercise 05
requires two real weeks.

### Part B — the metrics one-pager (about 20 min, once)

Before week 1 starts, draft your metrics one-pager per chapter
02. Three sections:

1. **Founder-numbers row.** Cash, monthly burn (gross and net),
   runway in months, default alive / default dead call. If you
   don't have real numbers for your context, use placeholders
   and note them.
2. **North-Star row.** Your chosen North-Star metric and two-to-three
   supporting inputs.
3. **This week's watch-list.** Two-to-three numbers that the
   week's plan should move (often tied to an OKR key result —
   exercise 04 covers the OKR).

Every number has three columns: current, last week (or best
estimate for week 0), target for this week.

You will update this one-pager Monday, Wednesday, and Friday of
each of the two weeks; ship the final Friday-week-2 version as
part of the deliverable.

### Part C — week 1 Monday plan (about 20 min)

Write the Monday plan for week 1, in the shape from chapter 02:

1. **This week's three-to-five things** — one sentence each,
   hours-of-attention budget next to each.
2. **On the metrics one-pager this week** — one line per number.
3. **Decisions expected this week.**
4. **What could take the week off-course.**

Ceiling: one page. Save it as `week-1-monday-plan.md` and
timestamp it.

### Part D — run the week (about 5 min at each check-in)

During the week, do the two things chapter 02 calls for:

- **Wednesday metrics check** (~10-15 min). Update the metrics
  one-pager with current values. If a number moved unexpectedly,
  write one sentence naming why on the one-pager.
- **Ad-hoc decision-log updates** (as calls happen). Add entries
  to the decision log per chapter 03. You do not need to hit a
  specific entry count for this exercise; the point is the
  logging habit, not the volume.

### Part E — week 1 Friday shipped-and-learned (about 40 min)

At the end of week 1, write the shipped-and-learned in the shape
from chapter 02:

1. **Shipped.** One line per shipped item (delivered, deployed,
   signed, closed).
2. **Learned.** Two-to-five sentences on what the week told you
   that you didn't know on Monday, anchored to specific numbers,
   customer signals, or log entries.
3. **What next week has to carry.** Roll-overs + new items.

Ceiling: one page. Save it as `week-1-friday-shipped.md` and
timestamp it.

### Part F — week 2 Monday plan (about 20 min)

Write the week 2 Monday plan, **explicitly derived** from week 1's
shipped-and-learned. Structural discipline: the "roll-overs" and
"new" items from week 1's Friday should appear (updated as
needed) as items on week 2's Monday plan. Not verbatim — the
world moves between Friday and Monday — but *traceable*.

Save it as `week-2-monday-plan.md` and timestamp it.

### Part G — run week 2 (mid-week check) (about 5 min)

Same Wednesday metrics check as week 1. Same ad-hoc log updates.

### Part H — week 2 Friday shipped-and-learned + reflection (about 40 min)

Write the week 2 Friday shipped-and-learned in the same shape as
week 1. Save it as `week-2-friday-shipped.md` and timestamp it.

Then write a **reflection on the interlock** — one page, three
sections:

1. **Where the interlock worked.** Which week 1 Learned items
   changed which week 2 plan items? Which week 1 roll-overs
   actually got picked up? What was the value of having the
   Friday shipped-and-learned as an input to Monday?
2. **Where the interlock broke.** Which week 2 plan items came
   from nowhere in week 1? Which week 1 items dropped without
   trace? What does the drop tell you?
3. **What you would change about the shape** of your Monday
   plan or Friday shipped-and-learned templates for your specific
   context. Chapter 02's shape is a starting point, not a
   frozen spec; two weeks of real use is where you find the
   parts that need to be tailored.

## Deliverable shape

A **folder** — `mod-004-exercise-05-cadence/` — containing five
files:

- `metrics-one-pager.md` (final Friday-week-2 version, with the
  full column history if you have it)
- `week-1-monday-plan.md`
- `week-1-friday-shipped.md`
- `week-2-monday-plan.md`
- `week-2-friday-shipped.md`
- `reflection.md` (the interlock reflection from Part H)

Total length: 1200–1800 words across all six files. Each individual
artifact is one page or less.

## Starter guidance

- **Commit to the two weeks before you start.** The exercise is
  worth doing when it's actually done across two real weeks. It
  loses most of its value if you retro-write both weeks on a
  single Sunday afternoon.
- **Start with a smaller Monday plan than you think.** Three
  items is fine. Four is fine. Six is too many — you will
  overrun and the shipped-and-learned's roll-over list will
  balloon. The one-page ceiling is the pressure that keeps the
  plan honest.
- **Write the shipped-and-learned on Friday, not on Sunday.**
  Sunday shipped-and-learneds are memory reconstructions; the
  specifics of Tuesday and Wednesday are already blurred. Friday
  is when the raw material is fresh.
- **Ship the artifacts as text files, not screenshots.** Whatever
  tool you actually use in your operating loop (Notion, Google
  Docs, plain files), for this exercise export or copy the
  content into text files that another reader can search. The
  discipline of the shape survives the tool transition; the
  screenshots don't.
- **Do not skip Wednesday.** Wednesday's metrics check is what
  makes Friday's Learned anchored to actual numbers. Without
  Wednesday, the Learned section drifts into vibes even in the
  real version.

## Acceptance criteria

You have completed the exercise when:

- [ ] Two consecutive real work weeks were run against the
      cadence (not simulated).
- [ ] A metrics one-pager in the three-section, three-column
      shape from chapter 02 is present.
- [ ] Week 1 Monday plan, week 1 Friday shipped-and-learned,
      week 2 Monday plan, and week 2 Friday shipped-and-learned
      are all present in the four-section / three-section shapes
      from chapter 02, each one page or less, each timestamped.
- [ ] Week 2's Monday plan is visibly traceable to week 1's
      Friday shipped-and-learned (roll-overs and new items map).
- [ ] A one-page reflection surfaces where the interlock worked,
      where it broke, and what you would change about the
      template shape for your context.
- [ ] The metrics one-pager was updated at least three times
      per week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday) in each of the two
      weeks.
- [ ] The folder is committed to your notes as
      `mod-004-exercise-05-cadence/`.

## Common failure modes to avoid

- **Retro-writing both weeks in one session.** Chapter 02's
  interlock only holds when the Friday shipped-and-learned is
  written before the next Monday plan. Retro-writing collapses
  the interlock into a single-day narrative and loses the
  entire drift-catching property.
- **Metrics one-pager that grows into a dashboard.** Chapter 02's
  ceiling is one page. If the one-pager has twenty rows by
  Friday of week 2, either the watch-list is bloating or the
  founder-numbers section is not doing its job.
- **Shipped section that includes "worked on."** "Worked on
  onboarding for four days" is not shipped; shipped means the
  thing left your desk. Work-in-progress goes in the
  "what next week has to carry" section, not in Shipped.
- **Learned entries that could have been written on Monday.**
  Learned is what the week told you *that you didn't know on
  Monday*. If a Learned entry is something you already believed
  going into the week, it's not really a Learned; cut it.
- **A week 2 Monday plan that ignores week 1's roll-overs.** The
  interlock is the whole point. If the week 2 plan has three
  brand-new items and no roll-overs, either week 1 finished
  everything (unusual) or the shipped-and-learned's roll-over
  section is being ignored (more common). Both should be
  visible in the reflection.

## What good looks like

Two consecutive weeks of artifacts a reader could pick up on
Sunday night, read in fifteen minutes, and see the *shape of two
weeks of real work* — what got planned, what actually shipped,
what got learned, what carried over, and how week 2 was
different because of what week 1 taught. The interlock is what
turns two weekly artifacts into a two-week arc.

The most useful signal is that **week 2's plan is not a copy of
week 1's plan**. If the two Monday plans are essentially the
same, either nothing changed (rare over two real weeks) or the
Friday shipped-and-learned isn't feeding the next Monday. The
whole point of the cadence is that the plan adapts to what the
week taught; if the plan doesn't adapt, the cadence is a ritual
without a purpose.

The cadence you exercise here is the same cadence the lab
(authored separately) asks you to run for one week, integrated
with the full loop. It is also the cadence a founder is
recommended to run for the rest of their career; the two-week
version here is the smallest window that surfaces the interlock
and the shape adjustments.
